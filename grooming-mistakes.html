7 Grooming Mistakes Most Pet Parents Make

BY SARAH JENNINGS, MASTER GROOMER • UPDATED MAY 2026

From improper undercoat brushing to dangerous water temperatures, how to avoid damaging your pet’s natural moisture barrier.

Home grooming strengthens the bond between human and pet, but well-intentioned mistakes can inadvertently damage protective undercoats, strip essential natural sebum oils, or cause painful matting burns.

1. Shaving Double-Coated Breeds

Shaving double-coated breeds like Huskies, Golden Retrievers, or German Shepherds does not keep them cool in summer. Their undercoat functions as thermal insulation against heat; shaving disrupts hair growth cycles and exposes sensitive skin to solar radiation and sunburn.

Essential Grooming Rules

  • Always Comb Before Bathing: Water tightens existing tangles into dense, painful pelts.
  • Use Lukewarm Water: Hot water dries canine epidermal lipid barriers and triggers intense itching.
  • High-Velocity Deshedding: Use forced-air dryers to dislodge loose undercoat rather than razors.

2. Bathing Before Dematting

Wetting matted fur acts like felting wool, tightening tangles into impenetrable pelts that pinch the skin and trap moisture against the dermis, creating ideal breeding grounds for fungal dermatitis.

3. Neglecting Quick-Safe Nail Trimming

Overgrown nails shift the angle at which paws meet the ground, forcing unnatural joint torque on pasterns, hocks, and hips. Trim nails bi-weekly in small increments to encourage the vascular quick to recede naturally.
