Senior Dog Nutrition: What Changes After Age 7

BY ELENA ROCHE, CANINE DIETITIAN • UPDATED MAY 2026

Adjusting protein ratios, moisture intake, and essential omega-3 fatty acids to protect renal function and combat muscle atrophy in senior dogs.

As dogs pass the age of seven, their basal metabolic rate naturally slows by approximately 20%, while their ability to synthesize joint lubrication and absorb essential minerals begins a steady decline. Continuing to feed adult-maintenance formulations frequently leads to obesity and accelerated arthritis.

1. Lean Muscle Preservation vs. Caloric Density

Senior dogs actually require higher proportions of digestible, high-biological-value animal protein to offset sarcopenia (age-related muscle wasting). Look for clean poultry or wild fish meals rather than plant-based concentrates or fillers.

Nutritional Checklist for Dogs Over 7

  • High-Biological Protein: Minimum 28–32% dry-matter protein from identifiable animal sources.
  • Hydration Enhancement: Adding warm bone broth or lightly hydrated food to support aging kidneys.
  • Glucosamine & Chondroitin: Daily therapeutic support to preserve synovial cartilage lubrication.

2. Targeted Omega-3 Fatty Acids (EPA & DHA)

Incorporating EPA and DHA Omega-3s acts as a systemic anti-inflammatory agent, directly reducing joint swelling and supporting cognitive brain health to combat canine cognitive dysfunction syndrome.

3. Fiber Optimization for Gut Motility

Digestive transit slows in older dogs. Incorporating moderate beet pulp, pumpkin, and soluble prebiotics promotes regular bowel movements without causing loose stools or nutrient malabsorption.
