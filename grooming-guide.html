09. COMPLETE PET GROOMING & COAT CARE GUIDE

GROOMING

Clean, Comfortable & Healthy

Grooming is much more than keeping your cat or dog looking clean. A good grooming routine maintains healthy skin and coat, keeps nails and paws comfortable, supports dental and ear hygiene, and gives you an opportunity to notice changes in your pet's body.

BY WE TWO PETS EDITORIAL TEAM

Every pet is different — breed, coat type, age, lifestyle, and temperament affect grooming frequency.

The Goal: Keep your pet clean, comfortable, healthy, and confident with regular, gentle care.

CHAPTER INDEX 10 TOPICS
CHAPTER 01 • BASICS

Understanding Pet Grooming

Clean Pet, Healthy Pet

Regular grooming allows you to check your pet from nose to tail. Changes and health abnormalities are often much easier to notice when you regularly handle and groom your pet.

WHILE GROOMING, INSPECT:

  • Skin & Coat: Flakes, redness, lumps, or parasites.
  • Eyes & Ears: Discharge, cloudiness, redness, or odour.
  • Teeth & Gums: Plaque accumulation, bad breath, or bleeding.
  • Nails & Paws: Overgrowth, cracked pads, or sensitivity.
START SLOWLY & REWARD

Touch → Grooming Tool → Short Session → Praise → Reward. Avoid forcing a frightened pet through a lengthy grooming session.

CHAPTER 02 • BATHING

Bathing & Coat Cleaning

Keeping the Coat Fresh & Comfortable

Bathing removes dirt, debris, odours, and outdoor contaminants. However, more bathing is not automatically better.

DOG BATHING WORKFLOW:

  • Before Bath: Brush out loose fur completely before wetting.
  • During Bath: Use comfortably warm water and dog-specific shampoo. Avoid getting water/soap in eyes and ears.
  • Rinsing: Rinse thoroughly until water runs completely clear. Leftover soap causes skin irritation.
  • Drying: Towel dry gently and keep warm until 100% dry.
CAT BATHING NOTE

Most healthy cats groom themselves effectively and rarely require routine baths unless they become unusually soiled with toxic/sticky substances.

CHAPTER 03 • BRUSHING

Brushing & Coat Maintenance

Healthy Coat, Less Loose Hair

Brushing removes shed hair, prevents painful mats, removes debris, and distributes natural protective skin oils evenly.

Coat Type Maintenance Focus Recommended Tools
Short Coats Weekly brushing to remove dead dander and loose fur. Rubber curry brush, bristle brush, grooming glove.
Double / Long Coats Daily to 3x weekly brushing behind ears, armpits, and underbelly. Slicker brush, undercoat rake, wide-tooth metal comb.
NEVER CUT TIGHT MATS CLOSE TO SKIN

Mats pull skin upwards into the knot. Cutting mats with scissors often results in severe skin lacerations. Use a detangling spray or visit a professional.

CHAPTER 04 • DERMATOLOGY

Skin Care & Common Problems

Look Beneath the Coat

A beautiful coat starts with healthy skin. Watch for dryness, redness, hot spots, scabs, and parasites during your regular brushing routine.

POST-OUTDOOR INSPECTION:

After park walks, woods, or muddy trails, inspect: Coat → Skin → Underbelly → Paws → Between Toes for ticks, foxtails, or thorns.

CHAPTER 05 • EAR HYGIENE

Ear Cleaning & Ear Care

Clean Ears Without Over-Cleaning

Healthy ears are pale pink, clean, and free of strong odours. Check weekly, especially for floppy-eared breeds and swimming dogs.

  • Use only veterinary-formulated ear cleaning solutions.
  • Massage base of ear for 20–30 seconds after applying cleanser.
  • Wipe away loosened debris gently with cotton pad.
  • Never insert cotton swabs / Q-tips deep into the ear canal.
CHAPTER 06 • FACIAL CARE

Eye & Face Cleaning

Gentle Care Around Sensitive Areas

Use a clean, damp cloth to gently wipe away crusting or tear stains from the inner corner outward. Clean facial folds on flat-faced breeds daily to prevent yeast infections.

CHAPTER 07 • ORAL HEALTH

Dental & Oral Care

Clean Teeth & Healthy Gums Matter

80% of dogs and 70% of cats show signs of periodontal disease by age 3. Daily or regular brushing preserves oral health and organ longevity.

NEVER USE HUMAN TOOTHPASTE

Human toothpaste contains detergents and xylitol sweeteners that are toxic and irritate pet stomachs. Always use enzymatic pet toothpaste in poultry or malt flavors.

CHAPTER 08 • PAWS & NAILS

Nail, Paw & Hygiene Care

Comfort for Everyday Walking

Overgrown nails alter paw biomechanics and cause skeletal strain. Trim nail tips every 2–4 weeks or use a silent rotary nail grinder.

  • Inspect paw pads for cuts, cracks, or embedded stones.
  • Trim hair between toe pads to prevent slipping on smooth floors.
  • Keep styptic powder handy in case a nail is accidentally cut too close.
CHAPTER 09 • SCHEDULES

Grooming Routine & Schedule

Making Grooming a Calm Everyday Habit
Frequency Tasks
Daily / Walk Return Eye wipe, paw inspection, quick brush for long coats, dental chew/brushing.
Weekly Thorough undercoat brushing, ear check/clean, full skin & lump examination.
Monthly / As Needed Nail trim, bath (dogs), sanitary hygiene trim, professional groomer visit.
CHAPTER 10 • SAFETY & PRODUCTS

Grooming Products & Safety

Using the Right Tools & When to See a Professional

Choose high-quality, ergonomic grooming tools suited specifically to your pet's coat length and species.

WHEN TO CHOOSE A VET INSTEAD OF A GROOMER

Contact a veterinarian for sudden alopecia, open hot spots, ear infections with pungent discharge, persistent bleeding gums, or suspicious skin masses.
