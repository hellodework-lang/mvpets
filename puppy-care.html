CHAPTER 01 • DEVELOPMENT

Understanding Your Puppy

Growing, Learning & Discovering the World

Puppyhood is one of the fastest periods of physical and behavioural development in a dog's life. Your puppy is learning every single day — where to sleep, where to go to the toilet, what they are allowed to chew, how to interact with people, and which behaviours get rewarded.

COMMON PUPPY BEHAVIOURS YOU MAY NOTICE:

  • Bursts of energy: Sudden playful "zoomies" followed by deep sleep.
  • Frequent sleeping: Growing bodies need massive recovery time.
  • Chewing and mouthing: Exploring their environment and soothing gums.
  • Curiosity: Investigating every corner, scent, and object.
  • Short attention spans: Easily distracted during early learning.
  • Fear or uncertainty: Caution around new or unexpected sounds.
  • Rapid changes in size and appetite: Steady physical expansion.
THE IMPORTANT PART

Your puppy is not born knowing your household rules. Teach the behaviours you want with patience, consistency, positive reinforcement, and realistic expectations. A puppy who feels safe and understands what is expected of them has a stronger foundation for adulthood.

CHAPTER 02 • VETERINARY CARE

Puppy Health & Veterinary Care

Building a Healthy Foundation Early

Veterinary care should begin early. A veterinarian assesses overall health, growth, weight, teeth, skin, coat, eyes, ears, heart, joints, and skeletal development.

KEY AREAS TO MONITOR:

  • Appetite: Learn what is normal. Persistent lack of appetite in a young puppy requires prompt attention.
  • Weight & Growth: Steady, healthy weight gain matching their expected breed size.
  • Digestion: Stool consistency and frequency. Watch for vomiting or diarrhea.
  • Eyes & Ears: Check for discharge, redness, odor, or frequent scratching.
  • Skin & Coat: Look for excessive itching, bald spots, redness, or parasites.
  • Energy: Look for bright, alert periods between normal long naps.
WHEN SOMETHING FEELS WRONG

Contact your veterinarian promptly if your puppy develops sudden changes in breathing, drinking, coordination, urination, or displays unusual lethargy. Very young puppies can become unwell quickly.

CHAPTER 03 • PREVENTIVE CARE

Vaccinations, Deworming & Prevention

Protection Starts Early

Puppies need preventive healthcare because their immune systems are still developing. Your vet creates an individualized preventive care plan.

Preventive Care Why It Matters Typical Schedule
Vaccination Series Protects against Parvovirus, Distemper, Hepatitis, and Rabies. Core series given every 3–4 weeks from 6–8 weeks up to 16 weeks of age.
Deworming Treats common intestinal parasites passed from mother or environment. Regular intervals starting in early puppyhood based on veterinary guidance.
Flea & Tick Prevention Protects against painful bites, skin allergies, and vector diseases. Use only products approved for your puppy's specific age and weight.
Heartworm Prevention Protects against life-threatening mosquito-transmitted parasites. Commence upon veterinary recommendation based on geographical region.
KEEP A PUPPY HEALTH RECORD

Maintain a journal recording vaccination dates, deworming, monthly weight, and vet visits. Never give human medication to a puppy unless explicitly instructed by a vet.

CHAPTER 04 • HYGIENE & GROOMING

Care & Grooming

Start Gentle, Start Early

Grooming is about much more than appearance — it helps you monitor skin, coat, paws, ears, and teeth while building lifelong tolerance for gentle handling.

  • Brushing: Choose a soft brush suitable for their coat type. Keep sessions short and rewarding.
  • Bathing: Use a gentle puppy-formulated shampoo with comfortably warm water and a non-slip bath mat. Avoid over-bathing.
  • Nail Care: Handle paws daily. Trim only tiny tips to prevent cutting the quick.
  • Ear Care: Check for dirt, redness, or wax. Never insert cotton buds deep into ear canals.
  • Dental Care: Introduce canine enzyme toothpaste gradually. Never use human toothpaste.
  • Paw Checks: Inspect between pads for cuts, thorns, or irritation after outdoor walks.
THE PUPPY GROOMING RULE

Short → Gentle → Positive → Reward. Never turn grooming into a stressful struggle; keep sessions under 3 minutes with generous treats.

CHAPTER 05 • NUTRITION

Food, Nutrition & Feeding Routine

Fuel for Healthy Growth

Nutrition during puppyhood fuels bones, muscles, brain development, and immune health. Feed complete and balanced growth diets tailored to your puppy's adult size.

FEEDING FREQUENCY BY AGE:

  • 8–12 weeks: 3–4 measured meals per day.
  • 3–6 months: 3 measured meals per day.
  • 6–12 months: Transition gradually to 2 meals per day.
Time Action Notes
7:00 AM Breakfast & Water Offer measured portion after morning potty break.
11:00 AM Second Meal Followed promptly by outside toilet opportunity.
3:00 PM Third Meal Consistent portion; ensure fresh water access.
7:00 PM Dinner Final planned meal allowing digestion before sleep.
FOODS TO KEEP AWAY FROM PUPPIES

Strictly avoid chocolate, grapes, raisins, onions, garlic, xylitol sweeteners, cooked bones, alcohol, and caffeine. Keep household chemicals safely stored.

CHAPTER 06 • POTTY HABITS

Toilet & Potty Training

Build the Habit Through Routine

Young puppies have small bladders and minimal physical sphincter control. Success comes from regular, predictable opportunities.

TAKE YOUR PUPPY OUT:

  • Immediately after waking up
  • 10–20 minutes after eating or drinking
  • Immediately after vigorous play or training
  • Immediately upon waking from naps
  • Right before bedtime
  • Whenever you notice sniffing, circling, or restlessness
POTTY TRAINING FORMULA

Opportunity → Correct Location → Toilet → Immediate Reward → Repeat. If an accident happens, clean thoroughly with enzymatic cleaner without anger. Punishment only creates fear of toileting near humans.

CHAPTER 07 • LEARNING

Training & Behaviour

Teach the Dog You Want to Live With

Training starts the day your puppy arrives. Keep sessions brief (2–5 minutes), fun, and centered entirely on positive reinforcement.

FOUNDATIONAL SKILLS:

  • Name Recognition: Saying their name and rewarding eye contact.
  • Recall Foundation (Come): High-value treats for returning to you happily.
  • Sit & Settle: Rewarding four paws on the floor and quiet resting.
  • Drop It & Leave It: Trading items for high-value alternatives without conflict.
  • Loose-Lead Walking: Rewarding walking by your side without pulling.
THE GOLDEN RULE OF TRAINING

Show → Reward → Repeat. Catch your puppy doing good things (resting quietly, chewing their toy) and reward them before bad habits develop.

CHAPTER 08 • SOCIALISATION

Exercise, Play & Socialisation

Build Confidence, Not Exhaustion

Socialisation means helping your puppy build calm, positive associations with the human world — not overwhelming them with chaotic environments.

  • Sensory Exposure: Vacuum sounds, umbrella opening, doorbells, lawnmowers, traffic from a safe distance.
  • Different Surfaces: Grass, gravel, tiles, wood, carpet, wet pavement.
  • Calm Introductions: Meeting friendly, vaccinated dogs and gentle people at their own pace.
  • Sniff Walks: Mental stimulation from sniffing is often far more satisfying and calming than strenuous endurance walking.
QUALITY OVER QUANTITY

Your puppy does not need to greet every dog or stranger. Calmly observing things from a safe distance builds reliable neutral confidence.

CHAPTER 09 • SLEEP & REST

Sleep & Daily Routine

Growing Puppies Need 16–20 Hours of Rest

Puppies sleep up to 18–20 hours a day. Sleep is crucial for memory consolidation, physical tissue growth, and emotional self-regulation.

OVERTIRED PUPPY SYMPTOMS

When puppies get overtired, they rarely go to sleep on their own — they become hyperactive, nippy, bitey, and irritable. Scheduled crate or pen naps in a quiet, dark room work wonders.

SAMPLE DAILY RHYTHM:

Morning: Wake → Potty → Breakfast → Potty → 10-min Play → 2-hour Nap.
Afternoon: Potty → Lunch → Training game → Potty → Long Nap.
Evening: Potty → Dinner → Family cuddle → Potty → Bedtime.

CHAPTER 10 • TEETHING

Teething & Chewing

Redirecting Natural Exploration

Between 3 to 6 months, puppies lose 28 baby teeth and grow 42 adult teeth. Chewing relieves inflamed gum pressure and releases calming endorphins.

  • Provide Variety: Natural rubber toys, textured nylon bones, chilled teething rings.
  • Cold Relief: Damp frozen washcloths or frozen carrot chunks soothe aching gums.
  • Calm Redirection: Whenever puppy teeth touch skin or furniture, calmly say "no" and swap in an approved chew toy immediately.
CHAPTER 11 • SAFETY

Home Safety & Puppy-Proofing

Viewing Your Living Space from Puppy Height

Puppies explore primarily with their mouths. Prevention through environmental management prevents emergency vet visits.

  • Tuck away or protect all electrical cables and power strips.
  • Remove toxic houseplants (Lilies, Sago Palm, Philodendrons).
  • Secure cleaning detergents, medicines, and trash bins with tight lids.
  • Keep shoes, socks, children's toys, and small swallowable items off floors.
  • Install baby gates to block staircases and open balconies.

CHAPTER 13 • CHECKLISTS

Everyday Puppy Checklist

Daily, Weekly & Periodic Routines

DAILY CHECKLIST:

  • Fresh drinking water in clean stainless steel bowl
  • Measured growth meals given on regular schedule
  • Regular potty breaks after naps, meals, and play
  • Two short 5-minute positive training sessions
  • 16+ hours of restful sleep in quiet area

WEEKLY CHECKLIST:

  • Full coat brushing & skin inspection
  • Check ears for odour, wax, or redness
  • Paw handling & nail tip maintenance
  • Wash bedding & disinfect food bowls
CHAPTER 14 • FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Common Questions From New Pet Parents
Why does my puppy bite my hands so much?

Puppies explore the world with their mouths and use biting during littermate play. When excited or overtired, this intensifies. Immediately redirect to a chew toy and enforce quiet nap times.

How much exercise does my puppy really need?

A standard rule of thumb is 5 minutes of structured exercise per month of age up to twice daily. Free play on soft grass is preferred over long forced sidewalk walks.

When can my puppy meet other neighborhood dogs?

Only after completing their core vaccination series (typically around 16 weeks). Until then, socialize in controlled environments with known, fully vaccinated, gentle adult dogs.
